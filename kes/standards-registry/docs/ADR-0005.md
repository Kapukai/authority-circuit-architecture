# ADR-0005 — Deterministic Allocation, Agent-Assisted Derivation

## Status

Candidate.

## Decision

ACA identifier allocation is a KES-governed registry operation. Agents are
bounded proposers and verifiers, not naming authorities. Work packages are the
unit of execution, review, and acceptance.

The numbering hierarchy is structural:

- `ACA-000` governs the program;
- decade identifiers such as `ACA-120` identify canonical standards modules;
- identifiers inside an owned decade, such as `ACA-121` through `ACA-129`, are
  children of the decade owner and require an explicit `parent_id`;
- an unused number is not automatically available merely because it is next.

## Rationale

Language models are useful for discovery, comparison, drafting, dependency
analysis, and adversarial review. They are not reliable concurrency controls.
Stable identifiers require a deterministic registry, collision checks, explicit
parentage, and human authorization.

## Consequences

Agent output remains non-canonical until accepted. KES can reject malformed or
colliding work before it reaches the standards layer. Human authority remains
separate from automated verification.

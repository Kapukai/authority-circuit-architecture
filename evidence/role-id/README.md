**Purpose:** Preserve documented cases relevant to the relationship between official status, evidence, safety, and independent scrutiny in public decision architecture.

## Central proposition

**An official role can establish powers and duties. It cannot, by itself, establish truthfulness, personal safety, or the correctness of a decision.**

The two cases below are counterexamples to an unconditional assumption that holding a protective or judicial role guarantees safe conduct. They support designing safeguards that remain effective when an official commits wrongdoing. They do not, on their own, establish that an institution favored the official's account, discriminated against a complainant, concealed misconduct, or corrupted a particular public decision.

## KGL-RID-002 — Jeremy Francis Plonski

**Source publisher:** U.S. Attorney's Office, District of Minnesota, U.S. Department of Justice.  
**Publication and update date:** September 8, 2026.  
**Source checked:** September 10, 2026.  
**Source type:** Official prosecutorial press release reporting a sentence and describing court documents. The underlying judgment and plea documents were not retrieved for this register.

[Official DOJ sentencing release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-mn/pr/minnesota-state-trooper-sentenced-27-years-imprisonment-production-and-distribution)

### Facts reported by DOJ

- Jeremy Francis Plonski received 324 months in prison, equivalent to 27 years, followed by 180 months, equivalent to 15 years, of supervised release.
- U.S. District Judge Kate M. Menendez imposed the sentence on September 8, 2026. His guilty plea was entered on October 8, 2025.
- DOJ describes 23 videos produced from approximately April through October 2023. It reports that he personally abused a minor while wearing his State Trooper uniform in some recordings and distributed recordings through a social media application.
- The Minnesota State Patrol terminated his employment. DOJ identifies the FBI and Minnesota Bureau of Criminal Apprehension as investigators, with assistance from the State Patrol and other partners.

**Preserved source excerpt:**

> “wore his uniform while personally sexually abusing a minor victim.”

### Supported use

This is direct support for the limited proposition that possessing an official protective role, and visibly displaying it, does not guarantee protective conduct. The prosecution and sentence also document accountability mechanisms operating in this case. They do not establish whether earlier safeguards were adequate or how quickly they acted.

**Suggested index terms:** role ID; official status; uniform; failed safety assumption; criminal misconduct by an officeholder; independent scrutiny; child protection; decision architecture.

**Research questions, not findings:** Did any prior decision rely on official status as a credibility shortcut? Was any report discounted or delayed? Did supervisory arrangements impede independent review? The release does not answer these questions.

## KGL-RID-001 — James Benjamin Gosnell Jr.

**Source publisher:** U.S. Attorney's Office, District of South Carolina, U.S. Department of Justice.  
**Publication and update date:** July 2, 2026.  
**Source checked:** September 10, 2026.  
**Source type:** Official prosecutorial press release reporting a guilty plea. The underlying plea documents were not retrieved for this register. No later sentencing status is asserted here.

[Official DOJ guilty plea release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sc/pr/former-charleston-county-magistrate-pleads-guilty-possessing-distributing-child-sexual)

### Facts reported by DOJ

- Former Charleston County magistrate James Benjamin Gosnell Jr. pleaded guilty to possessing and distributing child sexual abuse material.
- A PayPal CyberTip concerning payments to a known vendor led to an HSI investigation and a search of his residence on September 16, 2025.
- DOJ reports a confession and forensic confirmation of thousands of files.
- DOJ describes distribution of material to another individual in Florida in 2024.

### Supported use

This case is a counterexample to treating judicial office as a guarantee of safe personal conduct. The release does not establish that a judicial ruling was improper, that his office facilitated the offenses, or that another person's report was discredited because of his title. Payments for illegal material are not, by themselves, evidence of financial incentives corrupting a public decision.

**Suggested index terms:** role ID; judicial authority; failed safety assumption; criminal misconduct by an officeholder; evidence verification; independent investigation.

## Distinguishing the architecture claims

These distinctions are Kapukai analytical categories, not findings announced by DOJ.

| Proposed claim | Status supported by these sources | Evidence needed to extend the claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official status does not guarantee safe conduct | Supported by the documented cases | No broader claim about prevalence follows from two examples |
| A decision procedure gives greater factual credibility solely because of an official title | A failure mode to investigate; not established here | Decision records, stated reasons, policies, or controlled comparisons showing the effect of status while relevant evidence remains fixed |
| A complainant was treated adversely because of their role or identity | Not established here | Specific reports, responses, comparator evidence where available, and a traceable explanation of the different treatment |
| An institution concealed, enabled, or protected wrongdoing | Not established here | Evidence of institutional knowledge and the actions, omissions, instructions, or conflicts that affected the response |
| Financial incentives corrupted an official decision | Not established here | The actual incentive arrangement and evidence connecting it to the decision process or outcome |

“Role-based discrimination” is used here to identify a proposed form of unjustified differential treatment in evidence handling. It is not a conclusion that a legal discrimination claim has been established. Similarly, criminal wrongdoing by an official is not automatically proof of a public corruption offense or organization-wide corruption.

## Turning the concern into a decision architecture test

**Proposed test:** Keep the underlying evidence, relevant expertise, firsthand knowledge, and applicable decision criteria fixed. Vary an irrelevant prestige or role label. Check whether the system changes credibility, access to review, or protective action without an evidence-based reason.

Keep legally relevant powers and duties explicit. A test of factual credibility is different from a test of who is authorized to sign an order. Removing necessary context would confound the test.

Record:

1. What information entered the process and its source.
2. Which factual conclusion, classification, obligation, or enforcement action resulted.
3. Whether an official title changed the evidentiary assessment and why.
4. Who could independently examine the evidence and challenge an error.
5. Whether a complaint against an official required that official's permission to proceed.
6. What protection, correction, or review was available while the facts were disputed.

**Kapukai design requirement:** An official role must never substitute for evidence needed to justify a consequential decision. Scrutiny and protective review must remain available when the concern involves the person holding authority.

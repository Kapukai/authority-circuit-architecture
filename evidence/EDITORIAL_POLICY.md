# Editorial and correction policy

**Status:** Proposed policy for this working research draft.  
**Edition date:** September 10, 2026.

## Source and publication requirements

Use identifiable, accessible sources and link directly to the relevant material. Prefer judgments, orders, plea documents, official investigations, and official announcements; distinguish a prosecutor's description from an independently examined court document.

Each case entry records:
- its stable record ID and version;
- the source publisher, URL, publication date, and date checked;
- the event date and procedural stage reported by that source;
- whether the underlying court documents and present docket status were checked;
- facts reported, analytical interpretation, and limits;
- later developments and corrections, with dates and supporting sources.

A source check confirms the cited page was examined. It does not certify that the entire case record is complete or current.

## Historical record and current status

Write historical events as dated statements: “On [date], DOJ announced [event].” Use a separate dated field for verified later developments. Do not describe an entry as up to date merely because its original source remains online.

These initial entries are historical source summaries. Current docket status has not been independently checked, and no automated monitoring is configured.

Before using an entry in a new post, article, release, or decision analysis, check the originating source and reasonably available official case updates. If a material question cannot be resolved, label it unresolved and avoid presenting the old status as current.

## Corrections and material developments

When a credible correction or material development is received:
1. Record the affected entry and the supporting public source.
2. Verify the specific point. Do not demand unrelated private information from the person reporting it.
3. If the existing presentation may materially mislead while review is pending, place a visible review notice at the top and pause new promotion of the disputed claim.
4. Correct inaccurate wording or add the verified development prominently. Update any analytical conclusions that depended on the changed facts.
5. Record the date, reason, and source in the entry's change log. Preserve an accurate account of the historical sequence.

Examples include withdrawn pleas, altered or vacated judgments, acquittals, official source corrections, mistaken identity, and evidence that changes the register's stated inference. Later developments can require changing the interpretation even when an earlier event was accurately reported.

Use a visible correction in the current file; Git history alone is not a sufficient reader-facing correction. Where safe, retain superseded versions for accountability. Privacy incidents may require removal from repository history rather than ordinary version retention.

This draft does not promise continuous surveillance of cases or a response time that has not been operationally staffed.

## Submitting corrections

For corrections based entirely on public information, open an issue in this repository with the record ID, disputed sentence, and a link to the supporting public source. Avoid repeating unnecessary personal information.

Do not submit victim identities, private family documents, sealed material, home addresses, or sensitive attachments through public issues, pull requests, or commits. Obtain a separately agreed confidential channel before submitting any such material.

## Publication boundary

Include only necessary public facts and clearly marked analysis. Use restrained descriptions of abuse and preserve victim anonymity. Do not upload abuse imagery or recordings.

An individual's crime does not automatically establish organization-wide corruption, that a particular public decision was discriminatory, or that an official role caused the crime.

A draft label, citation, or correction policy does not itself provide legal immunity. These are editorial safeguards; applicable publication law depends on the jurisdiction and circumstances.

# AIM Research and Authority Boundary

## Allowed outputs

AIM may produce:

- candidate definitions;
- hypotheses;
- reference cases and counterexamples;
- measurement procedures;
- uncertainty statements;
- sensitivity analyses;
- reproducibility reports;
- adversarial findings;
- failure-mode catalogs;
- recommendations for further research.

## Prohibited interpretations

An AIM output must not be represented as:

- a legal conclusion;
- an authorization;
- proof of legitimacy;
- proof of wrongdoing;
- a final credibility judgment;
- a sufficient basis for irreversible action;
- a substitute for missing evidence;
- a certification unless a separate certification program exists.

## Hard-gate rule

A candidate hard gate identifies a condition that must be addressed before a profile may be treated as decision-relevant. A failed or indeterminate hard gate cannot be offset by strengths elsewhere in the profile.

Candidate hard-gate domains may include:

- identity and object-of-action integrity;
- provenance and evidence sufficiency;
- notice and opportunity to challenge;
- authority-chain support;
- proportionality to foreseeable burden;
- reversibility or containment;
- accessible remedy;
- unresolved material uncertainty.

The existence, wording, and thresholds of any gate remain research questions until validated.

## Anti-score rule

AIM must not output a single aggregate percentage such as “84% lawful,” “92% legitimate,” or “73% safe.”

Permitted forms include:

- gate state: PASS / FAIL / INDETERMINATE;
- measurement profile with units and uncertainty;
- explicit contradiction set;
- scenario-dependent sensitivity range;
- structured explanation of unsupported assumptions.

## Human control

Only an accountable human may:

- accept a research result;
- authorize a pilot;
- approve publication;
- propose standardization;
- authorize an operational use;
- decide action in an individual matter.

## Misuse response

If an AIM construct is used outside its validated scope, the result must be marked invalid for that use. The research record should preserve the misuse, affected assumptions, likely consequences, and containment action.
